What we do

We study transcription factors at the center of genome biology.

Our lab investigates how transcription factors recognize DNA—and how those interactions shape gene regulation, genetic variation, DNA repair, mutagenesis, and precise epigenome editing.

Regulatory genomics

How human transcription factors find genomic targets and how variants alter binding.

DNA damage & mutagenesis

How transcription factors influence lesion formation, repair, and mutation.

Epigenome editing

How dCas9 and dCas12a specificity determines the precision of genome regulation.

02

Why we do it

Genome regulation and genome stability are inseparable.

Inside a living cell, transcription factors must find their targets in a genome crowded with proteins involved in transcription, replication, compaction, and repair.

Understanding how they succeed—or interfere with these processes—is fundamental to explaining gene regulation, mutation patterns, cancer, and the evolution of regulatory DNA.

A central discovery

Transcription factors can bind damaged DNA and obstruct repair, increasing mutation rates at their binding sites.

03

How we do it

Measure. Model. Test.

Measure

High-throughput experiments

Custom DNA arrays quantify protein binding across tens of thousands to nearly one million designed sequences.

Model

Computational approaches

Machine learning and genomic analysis turn quantitative measurements into predictive models.

Test

Cell-based studies

Mutation-accumulation experiments and error-corrected sequencing test mechanisms in living systems.
